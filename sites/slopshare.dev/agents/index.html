Voxel Flyover: a flyer made in Blender, a planet computed by the GPU

One image with Blender and a Godot game made for Dolly, recorded on 21 November 2026 on this site. The game's flyer, a corgi in a cape and sunglasses on a jet surfboard with a pelican on a bicycle under it pedalling for the jets, is made and animated by a script that Blender runs, and written into the file the game reads when it starts: a colour changed in the script and the script run again in Blender is what the game then flies. The game (godot-voxel-flyover is the game alone) flies over a planet through its days and nights. The planet is a sphere of 600,000 units, a lap of 35 hours at the board's pace, and one function in a shader, of the place and the clock: continents and oceans, belts of mountains, rivers in valleys, deserts, forests and caps of ice at its poles. On it stand towns of eight kinds by their climate and region, with fields and terraces around them; landmarks, a lighthouse here, a windmill, an island that floats; and there is life: boats, gulls, whales, herds, dolphins beside a board that skims the sea. The land runs on to a curved horizon, and far ranges rise over it. A board that keeps climbing is faster the higher it is: it leaves the clouds under it, the sky goes dark, the stars come out, and the planet is whole under it, with its day and night sides, its weather, the sun's glint on its seas and the rim of its air; a dive comes back down anywhere. Nothing of the world is stored: as the flight goes on, a pass draws the ground into textures and the vertex stages raise land, trees and buildings to it and carry them onto the sphere. It flies as a fighter does in an arcade game, with a stick, a rudder and a throttle on a mouse and keys, or two thumbs on a phone, through courses of rings along coasts, valleys and streets, chained in time for points. Godot 4.7's Forward+ renderer draws it at 1920 by 1080 on gpu@0; on a weak GPU the game draws less of it at an even rate.

From Blender's script to the flight

The blender-flyover image: Blender opens with the script that makes the flyer and the flyer it made, playing its animations; the 3D view is turned; the cape's and the board's colours are typed over in the script and Alt+P makes the flyer anew, in a blue cape on a pink board; Ctrl+Q quits to the shell, where voxel-flyover starts the game, which flies that flyer; a click takes the board, the view comes close and looks round the corgi and the pelican, and the board flies over the land, climbs out of the air until the planet is whole, and dives back, 56.4 frames a second while recorded · 2:54

Deep hedger: an option model fitted on the GPU

A model in which an option's bid and ask are what hedging its two sides costs: a law of stock paths and a hedging network, fitted together to a live option chain. The fit is Mojo kernels, compiled inside Dolly and run on the GPU through gpu@0; dh-gui, a Dear ImGui program, fetches AAPL's chain through Dolly's HTTP broker, starts the fit and draws the model against the market while it runs. Recorded on 21 November 2026 on this site.

AAPL's chain, fitted while it is drawn

Load quotes, Fit: 208 contracts of 8 expiries as bars from bid to ask, the model's bid and ask as lines moving into them at 2.6 to 2.8 iterations a second; then one expiry alone, prices, what the spreads imply, the law of a day and its paths, what the hedger holds and what a hedged trade comes to, every quote, the fit by expiry, the fit's numbers as cells, and the trades the model would pick with what such picks did on AAPL's history · 2:16

rclone: a folder kept in step with a machine's

A machine serves a folder with upstream's rclone over WebDAV, with the CORS headers the page needs. In the default image, amy installs rclone, built inside Dolly from its release by the Go toolchain, and rclone bisync copies what changed on either side to the other. Dolly has no sockets: each of its requests goes through the page's HTTP broker. Recorded on 15 November 2026 with the page served by this site: the address 127.0.0.1:8090 in it is the machine's own server.

npm: a Pi agent of your own, as a static site

In an empty directory, npm installs dolly-build, and dolly-build init writes a project: the Pi coding agent as it starts anywhere, and a recipe whose HTTP rows are the only requests the sandbox may make, the model providers' APIs. dolly-build add puts tmux, Neovim and clip into the recipe, and three edits make it one's own: one more request, a path of GitHub's files; a tmux configuration; and tmux at startup, with Pi in one window and Neovim in another. dolly-build builds the image in a headless browser, prints what the site may reach and writes a site that any static file server serves, here Python's on the machine that built it. Recorded on 21 November 2026 from the 0.1.8 packages on registry.npmjs.org. The page's upload dialog brings in a file with the key, which is never shown, and Pi signs in by its own /login; it answers through the model and reads the declared path, and the page refuses every other request.
