# The test surface every page ships (`__dolly`, `terminal-text.mjs`, the shell's published result): the investigation

Read on `work/rust` (`integrate/round-1013`, head `e7a96cde`, 2026-10-14); history shared with `main`; tasks at `tasks/`. Line numbers of `src/`, `host/`, `abi/`, `include/`, `docs/`, `scripts/` and `test/` files are of that tree unless a commit is named. Nothing was built or run; every number comes from `git`, `grep` and the files. The sibling investigations (`supervisor-mirror`, `line-discipline`) are the model of this one and are not repeated.

## Summary

1. The published result is as old as Dolly. The first commit (`0d224977`, 2026-08-29) had `result_sequence` and `result_status` in the terminal mailbox, `dolly_terminal_publish_result` as a contract import, Slop calling it after every interactive line, and `window.__dolly = { worker, terminal, commandResults, foregroundPid, submit, input }`. They were the page's own way to drive its shell: the page ran a built-in regression suite on `?autorun=shell`, and an external CDP harness read `__dolly.commandResults`. No era of screen-scraping for command completion exists in the history; the screen reader (`visibleTerminalText`, `aa0e63c0`, 08-31) came for text, because Ghostty draws on a canvas (`18a78351`, 08-29).
2. One precedent of test logic leaving the page: `06369b4f` (09-07) took 244 lines (the in-page runner, `?autorun=shell`, `commandResults`) out of `src/browser.mjs` into `test/fixtures/`, with the doc line "there is no URL-triggered shell test runner. The harness drives normal image startup." The handles (`submit`, `input`, the two screen helpers) stayed. No commit or task tried injecting the handles; `addInitScript` has been used since `c70de9c5` (09-13) for `DOLLY_HTTP_POLICY`, a custom recipe and two GPU-worker rewrites, never for them.
3. The surface has been named as a question four times and decided never: `audit-24` (09-30, open; its 10-06 measurement and step 2 is the reflection's step 1, word for word: "one harness file that every launch point injects (`addInitScript`)"; step 3: "either documented beside `DOLLY_HTTP_POLICY` and `DOLLY_HOST_MODULES`, or handed to an embedder's callback"), `core-polish` (10-01), `round-1005` item 7 ("embedding API or test-only"), `man-help`/`robots` (10-07: an agent driving the deployed site found `__dolly` undocumented; `llms.txt` now documents screenshots and `data-dolly-status` instead). No owner statement on it was found.
4. Today `__dolly` is `Object.create(host.page, {4 own members})` (`src/browser.mjs:225-229`), defined only on image pages (`terminal.html:120`) once the page is `ready`; `host.page` is assembled from each module's `page` record (`host/modules.mjs:114-117`) and emptied on detach (`:132`), so membership follows the enabled modules: 24 with all nine page-bearing modules, 20 on `default`. Every member reads page-internal state or does what a key, click or paste does; none opens a browser authority the user lacks. `saveSession(name)` saves without the click.
5. The result path: Slop `interactive()` calls `dolly_terminal_publish_result(last_status)` after every line, blank ones too (`src/slop.c:5269`); libc adapter `runtime-adapter.c:407-410`; kernel `terminal_packet` publishes unless descriptor 1 is a pty slave (`process-kernel.c:1499-1502`; no check of who calls); `src/dolly.c:44-50` stores, increments, notifies. Consumers: `submit()` (`host/input/input.mjs:284-290`) and the harness's `result(action)` (`test/browser.mjs:42-46`), both through `__dolly.terminal`. Nothing shows the status to a user. It is named in one product document (`docs/process-model.md:235-236`) and in the review map (`docs/browser-boundary.md:62`).
6. Counted: 63 test files reference `__dolly` (33 `test/`, 30 `demos/*/test/`); 17 of them only through the harness's `open()` helpers; 19 files launch a browser. Outside tests: `scripts/accept-release.mjs:61-63` (release acceptance, also run by `test/image-inventory-browser.mjs`), `scripts/published-version.mjs:144-145` (the deployed site, production bundles), `demos/browser.mjs:52-97` (the demo harness), three task-local scripts. No embedder, bridge, recording, Studio or session UI uses it. `submit`'s status is asserted at 227 of 244 call sites, 54 for a non-zero value.
7. Lines that exist for it: about 128 of page JavaScript (`terminal-text.mjs` 63, `input.mjs` 19, `runtime.mjs` 14, `browser.mjs` 6, `modules.mjs` 5, the nine `page` records 21) and about 25 of C and headers (`dolly.c` 9, `process-kernel.c` 4, `runtime-adapter.c` 4, `runtime.h` 3, `process.h` 1, `process-kernel.h` 1, the fixture stub 1), one line of Slop, two WAT globals, three generated constants.
8. The reflection's claims: the 24 members, the 63 files, the two scripts, `src/slop.c:5269`, `input.mjs:284` and "not reachable from Wasm" are true; "every page" is every image page; "consumed only by `submit()`" leaves out the harness's `result()` (same path); "produced only by Slop" is true of production code. Its `browser.mjs:226-230` is `:225-229` here.
9. On the cases: without the two words the harness learns a command ended from the screen (a selection gesture per poll; the flake task's recorded failure rates are of that path) or from a marker (a Slop change, hence seed), and the status from `echo $?`. The screen helpers and every status probe need page-internal objects (`host`, `builtSystemSnapshot` are module-scope `let`s, `browser.mjs:27,30`), so an injected module needs the accessors or a hook the page ships. Removing the enum value changes `process.h`'s layout identity (`scripts/build.sh:96-98`; `26277ef5`), the seed and every image.
10. Not found: any owner words on the surface; any measurement of `submit` against a screen-based wait; any try of injection; whether an injected module can reach the page's objects without a shipped hook (untried; by ES-module semantics it cannot reach the `let`s); whether a forged publish or two overlapping `submit`s ever produced a wrong status.

## 1. History

### 1.1 The prototype: the result words and `__dolly` on day one (`0d224977`, 2026-08-29)

- Terminal mailbox version 1 (`abi/dolly-terminal-0.wat:12-16` at that commit): "Its first seven little-endian atomic u32 values are input_read, input_write, input_wake, result_sequence, result_status, foreground_pid, and flags." Its header: "Input and command-result state live in the shared memory mailbox returned below."
- `dolly_terminal_publish_result` was an `env` import of the command contract (`abi/dolly-0.wat:304-305`; commands then ran inside the kernel's memory), implemented in `src/dolly.c:341-345` (store, add, `emscripten_atomic_notify`), declared in `include/dolly/runtime.h:33`, called at `src/slop.c:871`.
- The page (`src/browser.mjs` at that commit): `TerminalTransport` with `resultSequence = 3`, `resultStatus = 4`, `foregroundPid = 5` (`:31-33`); `currentResultSequence()` and `waitForResult(sequence)` (`:73-83`, the same loop as today's `runtime.mjs:41-47`); `submitInput(command)` (`:277-293`): read the sequence, set `data-dolly-command`, `terminal.input(command + "\r")` (xterm.js then), race `waitForResult` against a 60 s timeout, push `{ command, status }` to `commandResults`. `window.__dolly` (`:603-616`): `worker`, `terminal` (xterm.js), `commandResults`, `foregroundPid`, `submit`, `input`. Then: `if (location.search autorun === "shell") await runBrowserProof()` (`:618-620`), a regression suite that ran in the page.
- The harness (`scripts/browser-harness.mjs`, Chrome over CDP): polled `data-dolly-status` for `passed`/`failed` (`:316-320`), read `window.__dolly?.terminal?.options?.fontSize` around Ctrl+= and Ctrl+- (`:318-328`) and `results: window.__dolly?.commandResults ?? []` (`:354`).
- `AGENTS.md` at that commit already said "Verify changes in a real browser" and "Never add Node or native-host fallbacks to make a browser test pass"; "Avoid feature flags" came with `3e8150e2` (09-05).

So the result words were the page's first channel for "this command is done, with this status", and the first consumer was the page's own proof, not an external test.

## 2. What it is today

### 2.1 Where `__dolly` is defined and on which pages

`src/browser.mjs:225-229`, the last lines of `boot()`, after `data-dolly-status = "ready"` (`:222`):

```js
window.__dolly = Object.create(host.page, Object.getOwnPropertyDescriptors({
  ...terminalText(host.page),
  get hostModules() { return host.enabled; },
  get systemSnapshot() { return builtSystemSnapshot; },
}));
```

`host.page` is the object `host/modules.mjs:54` creates; `attach` copies each module instance's `page` record onto it by descriptor (`:114-117`, duplicates refused) and `detach` deletes those keys (`:132`), so a module `nena@0` enables or disables adds or removes members at run time (`test/nena-browser.mjs:138,154`). The prototype is live: `__dolly` sees the module set as it is.

`src/browser.mjs` is imported by one page template, `terminal.html:120`, which `scripts/generate-routes.mjs:28` renders per image route (`/IMAGE/`, `/IMAGE/rebuild/`, `/custom/…`, `/session/…`). The menu, licences, `/view/` and `/sessions/` pages have no runtime and no `__dolly` (`test/custom-session-browser.mjs:305` asserts `typeof __dolly === "undefined"` on `/sessions/`). A page that fails to boot, or a rebuild page of an image without `ENTRY` (`:128-137`, status `built`), never reaches `:225`.

Nine modules carry a `page` record; download, upload, threads, sockets, pty, dso, build, packages and nena carry none. With all nine: 4 + 2 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 24. `default` declares runtime, display, input, http, snapshot among its twelve (`boundary.md`, nena section): 20 members until `nena enable` adds one.

**Authority.** Every write member produces what a user's key, click, paste or Save does, through the same functions (`pushText`, `pushPaste`, `pushSyntheticKey`, `pushPointer`, `session.save`, `interruptForeground`); none reaches Fetch, a file, a device or a Worker. The difference from a user is the absence of the gesture: `saveSession(name)` without the dialog (boundary.md C1), `interruptForeground()` without the keydown, text records without the page's chord handling (`input("\x03")` is a byte, not Ctrl+C). The read members expose counters and status objects that have no page text (`httpRequestCount`, `gpu.stats`, `audio`, `systemSnapshot`) or exist as page text (the indicators).

**Who can reach it.** Any script in the page's realm: `page.evaluate` from Playwright, a same-origin script, a browser extension. Not Wasm, by reading: the runtime Worker's messages are dispatched by `message.type` to module handlers (`src/browser.mjs:155-170`, `host.handle`), none of which names `__dolly`; `src/runtime-worker.mjs`, `src/process-worker.mjs`, `host/dso/*.mjs` contain no `__dolly` or `window.`; the plugin loader refuses JS bindings (`src/kernel-plugin.mjs:38-42`). What Wasm can do is write the six mailbox words, which are in its memory: a process can call the publish operation with any status (§2.4), which resolves a waiting `submit` with that status.

### 2.3 `terminal-text.mjs` and `submit`, step by step

`src/terminal-text.mjs` (63 lines; header: "What tests and recordings read a terminal page with, over the page APIs of display@0, input@0 and the runtime") is imported once, by `src/browser.mjs:10`. It needs `page.transport`, `page.inputTransport`, `page.terminal` and nothing else.

- `waitFor(predicate, description, attempts = 500)` (`:7-14`): 10 ms polls; throws naming `description`, or returns false without one.
- `visibleTerminalText()` (`:22-39`): for each of four attempt budgets (25, 50, 100, 325 polls): read `geometry()` and `dimensions()` (returns `""` if the grid is empty); press at a quarter cell in, wait until `copySelection() === null` ("the press drops an earlier selection"), drag to the last cell, release; wait for a non-null selection; return it. Four failed gestures throw "timed out waiting for terminal selection publication".
- `waitForInteractiveTerminal(pattern, description, previousPid)` (`:43-60`): up to 6,000 polls: `pid = terminal.foregroundPid()`; false while `pid <= 0`, `pid === previousPid`, the foreground is interruptible (ISIG set, so a shell at a command or a cooked program), a program draws the canvas, a program holds the input lease, or the ring is not drained; then reads the screen and requires the pid and ISIG state unchanged and `pattern` to match. Then a click at the first cell and a wait for the ring to drain, to leave no selection. Returns the pid.

`submit(command, text = command + "\r")` (`host/input/input.mjs:284-290`):

1. `sequence = terminal().currentResultSequence()` (word 0, `runtime.mjs:37-39`).
2. `transport.pushText(text)`; a refused record throws "Dolly input mailbox is full".
3. Races `terminal().waitForResult(sequence)` (`Atomics.waitAsync` on word 0 until it differs, then returns word 1, `:41-47`) against a `stopped` promise kept in `waiting`; `dispose()` rejects every pending one with "Dolly stopped" (`:515-516`).

It knows nothing of which command ended: the next publish, whoever makes it, resolves it. Slop publishes after every interactive line, blank lines and comments included, and 130 after an interrupted line (`src/slop.c:5241-5269`, the publish is outside `if (report_status)`). Two `submit`s that overlap read the same sequence and resolve on the same publish; the demo harness relies on the first property for background jobs (`demos/browser.mjs:62-68`, `start(command)`: "status stays null until the command exits"). The harness's `result(action)` (`test/browser.mjs:42-46`) is the same wait around an action of the test's own: a typed line through Playwright's keyboard (`test/site-browser.mjs:135-137`, `test/buttons-browser.mjs:82`), a paste chord (`test/terminal-browser.mjs:49,53`), a button click (`buttons-browser.mjs:78-172`), editing keys (`shell-browser.mjs:49`), an IME event (`:131`).

## 5. What a change would mean, on real cases

Observations only. "Today" is the code above. "Alternative" is the reflection's two steps: (1) `terminal-text.mjs`, the four input members and `__dolly` itself defined by a module the harness injects (`addInitScript`); (2) `TERMINAL_PUBLISH_RESULT`, `dolly_terminal_publish_result` and the two words gone from `process.h`, `runtime.h`, the kernel, the WAT and Slop.

### (a) "Type a command and wait for its status": `submit`

Today: one text record, one `Atomics.waitAsync`, the status word; no screen access, no frame drawn for the wait; 244 call sites in 56 files, 227 asserting the status. Under (1) alone, `submit` keeps its shape in the injected module: it needs `inputTransport.pushText` and `terminal.currentResultSequence`/`waitForResult`, so `__dolly.inputTransport` and `__dolly.terminal` (or the mailbox's memory and address) stay reachable from the page (§5c).

Under (2), what the tree offers to know a command ended:

- The screen. `waitForText(prompt)` exists (`test/browser.mjs:48-54`, 9 files): a `visibleTerminalText()` every 50 ms, each a press, a wait for the old selection to go, a drag and a release, with up to four repeats; it leaves a selection on screen (`demos/browser.mjs:54-60` clicks it away). The prompt regex `/dolly:[^\n]*\$\s*$/` (`test/browser.mjs:25`) also matches the prompt that was on screen before Enter when a command prints nothing and nothing scrolls; a test would mark its own lines. The status would be read from the screen (`; echo STATUS=$?` or Slop's own `slop: status N` line, printed only for non-zero and never for 127). The gesture cannot run while a program holds the input lease (`terminal-text.mjs:49`), and it depends on the terminal's selection publication, whose recorded failures are in §1.9 (3 of 220 under load before the kernel fix; 6 of 10 at a Neovim exit before the repeat; the Emacs demo every second run in Firefox under load, open).
- A marker. Slop could print a sentinel (an OSC 133 prompt mark or a line) that the harness reads from the screen: still a Slop change, still the screen, and the text shows to users.
- The four product words. `foreground_interruptible` is 1 while Slop runs a command with ISIG set (`src/slop.c:5255-5257`) and 0 at its editor (`:5234`); a harness could wait for the 1-then-0 edge. It carries no status and misses a command that ends between two polls; nothing in the tree does this.

Overlap and forgery are the same under today's design and the alternative that keeps the words: no per-command identity, any process may publish.
